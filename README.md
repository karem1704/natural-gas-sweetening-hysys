# Natural Gas Sweetening Using Amine Absorption

## Project Overview

This project presents a process engineering study of natural gas sweetening using amine absorption, based on an industrial internship experience at ETAP (Entreprise Tunisienne des Activités Pétrolières).

The study focuses on the removal of acid gases, particularly carbon dioxide (CO₂) and hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), to improve natural gas quality.

## Objectives

- Understand the operating principles of amine-based gas sweetening.
- Study the absorber and amine regeneration sections.
- Explore the influence of operating parameters on acid gas removal.
- Compare amine solvents and discuss process performance.
- Identify opportunities for process optimization and energy efficiency.

## Process Description

The process consists of two main sections:

1. **Absorption:** Sour natural gas contacts an amine solvent in an absorber. CO₂ and H₂S are transferred from the gas phase to the liquid phase.
2. **Regeneration:** The rich amine is heated in a regeneration system to release the absorbed acid gases and recover the solvent for reuse.

## Tools and Methods

- Aspen HYSYS — process simulation
- Process flow analysis
- Mass and energy balance concepts
- Sensitivity analysis
- Technical comparison of amine solvents
